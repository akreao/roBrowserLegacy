## Thread: "Sprites disappear after context restoration" (red, src/Renderer/SpriteBatch.js / SpriteAtlas.js)

Fixed in 7156e503, together with #1519's 3a9aeefa:
- `Renderer.onContextRestored` calls `SpriteBatch.contextRestored()`. That drops the batch's program, VAO and instance buffer, and now also calls `SpriteAtlas.contextRestored()`.
- `SpriteAtlas.contextRestored()` drops both texture arrays and the palette texture, so the next `init()` creates them again under a new generation. It doesn't depend on whether the context object changed.
- With the new generation, each sprite file packs itself again on its next draw. The release callbacks left from the old generation do nothing. The palette rows start over.
- A test restores the context with the same context object. It checks that the VAO and palette texture are new, that the file is packed again, and that the sprite still draws. It fails without the fix.

As on #1519: on `master`, `SpriteRenderer`'s own program and quad buffer already stay on the lost context after a restore. That's outside this PR, so I haven't changed it.

## Thread: "Atlas growth doubles peak GPU storage" (observation, src/Renderer/SpriteAtlas.js, `allocate`)

That's right. Growing creates the larger array, copies the layers across, and only then deletes the old one. Here are the limits:
- **Palette-index array:** R8 at 2048², 4 MiB per layer. It starts at 2 layers and doubles up to 16 (64 MiB).
- **RGBA array:** RGBA8 at 1024², 4 MiB per layer. It starts at 1 layer and doubles up to 16 (64 MiB).
- **Palette texture:** 256 × 512 RGBA, 0.5 MiB.

The worst moment is the last growth of one array, 8 to 16 layers: 32 + 64 = 96 MiB for that array while the copy runs. The two arrays grow at different times. Both are also capped by `MAX_ARRAY_TEXTURE_LAYERS` and `MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE`.

I haven't measured peak GPU memory on a constrained device. If you'd rather keep that peak down, the simplest options are a lower `maxLayers` or growing by a fixed step instead of doubling. Frames that don't fit already fall back to their own textures, so a lower limit costs draws, not correctness.
