## Thread: FPS ceiling exceeded on faster displays (FramePacer.js, `refreshesPerFrame`)

This is deliberate. A 60 Hz panel commonly measures 60.0x Hz. Under a strict ceiling, those panels would be paced by time, and the time grid would drop one refresh every so often (about every 45 s at 60.02 Hz, more often with timestamp jitter). That is the stutter this PR removes. So the cap allows at most 1% over: 60.5 Hz with a 60 cap renders 60.5. 5bbe39c updates the header comment to say this instead of "never exceeded", and the existing "never renders faster than the limit" test already asserts the 1% bound. The fix for the previous thread doesn't widen it: a kept count is always at or above the one the 1% rule picks.

## Thread: Display changes retain old pacing samples (FramePacer.js, `reset`)

Leaving this as is. The estimate is the mean of the middle half of the last 31 intervals, so after a refresh-rate change it moves to the new rate once about 16 new refreshes are in, which takes a quarter of a second at 60 Hz. A blended value only appears for those few frames. Calling `reset()` on a display change would need a display-change signal the browser doesn't give. `reset()` stays for tests.
